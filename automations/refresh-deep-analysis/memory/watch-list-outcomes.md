# Watch-List Outcomes

Ledger of every `watchList` item from prior `DEEP_ANALYSIS` runs. **Every item carries a probability** (`p ∈ [0,1]`, rounded to 0.05). Scored once the `window` has elapsed with a Brier score.

**Outcomes:** `hit` (1.0) / `miss` (0.0) / `no-resolution` (the event text genuinely cannot decide it — no Brier, EXCLUDED from every calibration figure). There is no `partial`: a binary forecast either met its own stated threshold or it did not (rule changed OCT 3, 2026).

**Brier per item:** `(p − outcome)²` where outcome ∈ {0, 1}. Lower = better-calibrated.

**Workflow:**
- At start of run: score any unresolved items whose `window` has elapsed (or whose letter is already DETERMINED — a weekly-close row after the last Friday in its window; a "discloses X by date" row once X is filed). Compute Brier per resolved item, then update `calibration.md`.
- At end of run: append the new run's `watchList` items as unresolved with their probabilities.
- Decay: rows older than 26 weeks → archive (move to bottom under `## Archived`).

## Hygiene rules

1. **Every active item has a probability.** No "unrated" entries.
2. **Probabilities rounded to 0.05.** False precision (0.673) is noise.
3. **0.50 is a refusal** — split into sharper sub-questions or commit to a direction.
4. **Outcome assignment requires a primary-source citation** in the lesson cell. Don't score from press summaries alone.
5. **There is no partial credit (OCT 3).** A binary forecast is a hit or a miss against the threshold in its own event text and the cited primary source. If the event text genuinely cannot decide it, mark `no-resolution` and exclude it from all Brier and calibration math; never pick an outcome to move the number, and state the reasoning in one line.
6. **After scoring, update `calibration.md`.** Resolution without calibration update = run is incomplete.
7. **Every rendered scoring letter carries its own gloss (SEP 13).** `watchList[].event` and `falsificationConditions[]` are read-only to the Editor; a term a newcomer cannot parse there is the drafter's to fix, and the letter must stay scorable after the gloss.
8. **Never restate a row on the partial realisation of its own letter (SEP 26).** Restatements are for EXOGENOUS information; a multi-week row whose weeks have printed scores at the probability it carried before the first print. The THESIS probability (tracker) is where realised legs move a number; the row's plain line may say "on today's evidence it looks unlikely" (OCT 16) so the reader is not misled.
9. **Name the instrument in a rulemaking row (SEP 26) — and its review clock (OCT 7).** ANPRM / NPRM / final rule / guidance are different events on different clocks (EO 12866: a prerule 10 working days; rules 90 days).
10. **Never score a row logged after its resolving information was public (OCT 24).** If the outcome, or the vote or filing that decides it, was already public when the row was registered, mark it `no-resolution`: listed, never scored.
